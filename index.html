Technology

A resorbable fixation platform.

One material, applied at closure, designed to do the work that drains and tension sutures do today — and then disappear.

Mechanism Adhesion across an interface, not fixation at a point.

A quilting suture holds two tissue planes together at a series of points. Between those points, the plane stays open — and fluid finds it. Tissuelock is designed to hold the planes together everywhere they touch.

Form factor

A thin, conformable sheet handled like tape in the operative field — no mixing, no cure time to manage.

Interface

Engineered to bond to fascia and to the underside of the flap, across wet, moving, irregular tissue.

Lifetime

Designed to hold through the window when seroma forms, then resorb rather than persist as an implant.

The clinical need

A drain is a workaround, not a fix.

After abdominoplasty and other large-flap procedures, patients go home with tubes, bulbs, and a log book. Surgeons accept the burden because the alternative — fluid collecting in an open plane — is worse.

We think the better answer is upstream: remove the space instead of draining it.

About

The name is the whole thesis.

Tissuelock exists because the most common complication in large-flap surgery is a space, and spaces can be engineered away.

Origin Surgeons and materials scientists at the same table.

The company started from a complaint, not a compound. A plastic surgeon described the same post-operative routine he had run thousands of times — drains, logs, weekly visits — and asked why the field had accepted managing a problem instead of removing it.

We took that as a materials brief. Hold two wet, moving tissue planes together for the weeks that matter, add nothing to the operation, and leave nothing behind. Everything Tissuelock builds is measured against those three constraints.

Investors

A routine procedure with a standing tax.

Abdominoplasty is among the most common aesthetic procedures performed each year. Nearly all of it carries drains, follow-up, and a seroma risk everyone plans around.

Why now

Surgical adhesives have improved enough to hold living tissue, and resorbable chemistry has matured enough to disappear on schedule. The two curves crossed recently — which is why a fixation layer is buildable now and was not a decade ago.

We are pursuing a single, well-defined entry indication with a clear predicate landscape, then expanding into the broader set of procedures that leave a plane behind — mastectomy, latissimus harvest, hernia repair.

Entry indication Abdominoplasty

High volume, elective, and a procedure where the drain is the patient's main complaint.

Stage Pre-clinical

Bench characterisation complete; large-animal work under way.

Expansion Any large flap

Breast reconstruction, donor sites, and hernia repair share the same failure mode.
